Claude Code Was Steganographically Marking Requests — HN #1 (605 pts); Anthropic Acknowledged and Fixed in v2.1.197

A developer reverse-engineered Claude Code v2.1.196 on June 30 and found hidden Unicode steganography baked in since v2.1.91 (April 2): the date separator in 'Today's date is' flipped from dash to slash and the apostrophe cycled through four visually identical Unicode characters to silently encode whether the user's system timezone was Asia/Shanghai or Asia/Urumqi and whether their proxy hostname matched a hardcoded list of Chinese domains or AI-lab keywords. The post hit HN #1 at 605 points and 190 comments; The Register, Semafor, and The Information all covered it within hours. Anthropic confirmed it was 'an experiment to prevent account abuse from unauthorized resellers and protect against distillation' and said the fix was already merged — it shipped in v2.1.197.
